## Overview
To cut something off decisively; to make a clear break.

## Phrase
**一刀两断** — yī dāo liǎng duàn
## Characters


一 (yī) — one  
刀 (dāo) — knife  
两 (liǎng) — two  
断 (duàn) — to break


## Origin
The origin of this idiom comes from ancient practices where a knife was used to cut through a rope or material, symbolizing a clean break. In historical contexts, it was often used to describe the act of severing ties or ending relationships with resolution. This phrase encapsulates the idea that sometimes, to move forward, one must make difficult decisions and cut away the past.

## Examples
- 他决定和他的朋友一刀两断。<br>He decided to cut ties with his friend decisively.
- 在生意上，有时候需要一刀两断。<br>In business, sometimes you need to make a clean break.
- 他们的关系终于一刀两断了。<br>Their relationship finally came to a clear end.
