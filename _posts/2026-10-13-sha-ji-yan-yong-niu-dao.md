## Overview
Using a big tool for a small task.

## Phrase
**杀鸡焉用牛刀** — shā jī yān yòng niú dāo
## Characters


杀 (shā) — to kill  
鸡 (jī) — chicken  
焉 (yān) — why  
用 (yòng) — to use  
牛 (niú) — cow  
刀 (dāo) — knife


## Origin
This idiom originates from ancient Chinese agricultural practices. Farmers used large knives to butcher bigger animals, but such tools were unnecessary for smaller tasks like killing a chicken. The saying emphasizes the absurdity of using overly complicated solutions for simple problems. Over time, it became a metaphor for wasting effort or resources on trivial matters.

## Examples
- 你用那么复杂的方法来解决这个问题，杀鸡焉用牛刀。<br>You're using such a complicated method to solve this problem; it's like using a big knife to kill a chicken.
- 他花了几千块钱买工具来修电脑，真是杀鸡焉用牛刀。<br>He spent thousands on tools to fix the computer; that's really overkill.
- 对于这个小项目，雇个专家简直是杀鸡焉用牛刀。<br>Hiring an expert for this small project is just overkill.
