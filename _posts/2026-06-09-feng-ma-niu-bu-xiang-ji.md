## Overview
completely unrelated; like wind, horse, and cow not affecting each other

## Phrase
**风马牛不相及** — fēng mǎ niú bù xiāng jí
## Characters


风 (fēng) — wind  
马 (mǎ) — horse  
牛 (niú) — cow  
不 (bù) — not  
相 (xiāng) — mutual / each other  
及 (jí) — to reach / to relate


## Origin
This idiom originates from a classical Chinese text where it was used to emphasize the lack of connection between two subjects. The imagery of wind, horses, and cows illustrates how diverse and unrelated these elements are. It serves as a reminder that some topics or matters are so distant from each other that they cannot be connected at all.

## Examples
- 他们的兴趣风马牛不相及。<br> Their interests are completely unrelated.
- 这两个项目风马牛不相及。<br> These two projects are not related at all.
- 他的观点和我的风马牛不相及。<br> His viewpoint is entirely different from mine.
