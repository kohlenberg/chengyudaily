## Overview
To drive south while the wheels head north; to be completely off track.

## Phrase
**南辕北辙** — nán yuán běi zhé
## Characters


南 (nán) — south  
辕 (yuán) — to drive  
北 (běi) — north  
轼 (zhé) — wheels


## Origin
The idiom originates from a tale in ancient China where a man wanted to travel south, but instead of heading in the right direction, he drove his chariot north. His actions led him further away from his goal, illustrating how one can be misguided despite strong intentions. This story serves as a reminder to ensure our actions align with our goals.

## Examples
- 他想要当医生，但总是玩游戏，真是南辕北辙。<br>He wants to be a doctor, but always plays video games; he is really off track.
- 如果你不制定计划，努力工作也可能南辕北辙。<br>If you don’t make a plan, working hard might lead you in the wrong direction.
- 我们的项目没有明确目标，所以进展南辕北辙。<br>Our project lacks clear goals, so our progress is completely off track.
