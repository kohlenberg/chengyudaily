## Overview
Discussing military tactics on paper; to talk about theories without practical experience.

## Phrase
**纸上谈兵** — zhǐ shàng tán bīng
## Characters


纸 (zhǐ) — paper  
上 (shàng) — on/above  
谈 (tán) — to talk/to discuss  
兵 (bīng) — soldiers/military


## Origin
This idiom originates from the historical context of the Warring States period, where a general named Zhao Kuo was known for his theoretical knowledge of warfare. However, when he faced a real battle, his lack of practical experience led to his defeat. His reliance on strategy without hands-on skill became a cautionary tale.

## Examples
- 他的计划太理想化了，完全是纸上谈兵。<br>His plan is too idealistic; it’s all just talk without action.
- 不要纸上谈兵，快去实践你的想法。<br>Don’t just talk about theories; go put your ideas into practice.
- 这位教授的理论很精彩，但在实际应用中仍然是纸上谈兵。<br>The professor's theories are brilliant, but they remain just talk when applied in practice.
