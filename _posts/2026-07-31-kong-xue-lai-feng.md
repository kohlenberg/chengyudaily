## Overview
Empty holes bring wind; rumors often have some truth.

## Phrase
**空穴来风** — kōng xué lái fēng
## Characters


空 (kōng) — empty, void  
穴 (xué) — hole, cave  
来 (lái) — come  
风 (fēng) — wind


## Origin
This idiom originates from ancient Chinese literature, where it was noted that when the wind blows, it often finds an entry point through holes. It symbolizes that if there are whispers or rumors, they may not be baseless; there might be some truth hidden within them. The idea is that something cannot come from nothing, much like how a wind cannot blow without a source.

## Examples
- 这件事情大家都在讨论，看来空穴来风。<br>This matter everyone's discussing seems to have some truth behind it.
- 如果没有证据，别随便相信空穴来风。<br>If there's no evidence, don't believe every rumor you hear.
- 她的突然离开引起了很多猜测，真是空穴来风。<br>Her sudden departure sparked many speculations; there must be some truth to it.
