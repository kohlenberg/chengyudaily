## Overview
literal: soy sauce bottle; figurative: someone who is easily manipulated

## Phrase
**酱油瓶子** — jiàng yóu píng zi
## Characters


酱 (jiàng) — sauce  
油 (yóu) — oil  
瓶 (píng) — bottle  
子 (zi) — a suffix for objects


## Origin
In traditional Chinese homes, soy sauce was stored in bottles. These bottles would often be swayed back and forth, much like a person who simply follows the opinions of others without a firm stance. Over time, this image evolved into a metaphor for those who are indecisive or easily influenced. It’s a playful way to describe someone who doesn't stand firm on their own beliefs.

## Examples
- 你总是跟着别人的意见，真是个酱油瓶子。<br>You always follow others’ opinions; you really are a soy sauce bottle.
- 她在团队中就像酱油瓶子，缺乏主见。<br>She is like a soy sauce bottle in the team, lacking her own views.
- 不要当酱油瓶子，勇敢表达自己的想法。<br>Don’t be a soy sauce bottle; bravely express your own thoughts.
