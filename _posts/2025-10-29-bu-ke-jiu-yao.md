## Overview
beyond remedy; hopeless

## Phrase
**不可救药** — bù kě jiù yào
## Characters


不 (bù) — no; not  
可 (kě) — can; able  
救 (jiù) — rescue; save  
药 (yào) — medicine; remedy


## Origin
This idiom originates from ancient Chinese literature, where it was said that certain ailments could not be cured by medicine. Over time, it evolved to describe situations that are irreparable, whether in personal life or broader contexts. The idea is that once something is beyond help, no remedy can fix it. It serves as a cautionary reminder of the limits of intervention.

## Examples
- 他的习惯已经不可救药了。<br>His habits are already beyond remedy.
- 这个项目的损失不可救药。<br>The losses from this project are beyond repair.
- 他们的关系已经不可救药。<br>Their relationship is already hopeless.
