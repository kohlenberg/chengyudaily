> Ever wondered how gossip spreads through whispers?

## Overview
Hearsay or rumors; information not verified.

## Phrase
**道听途说** — dào tīng tú shuō
## Characters


道 (dào) — way or path  
听 (tīng) — to hear  
途 (tú) — road or route  
说 (shuō) — to speak or say


## Origin
This idiom originates from ancient times when travelers would share stories they heard during their journeys. Often, these tales were exaggerated or distorted, leading to misinformation. Thus, it cautions against believing everything you hear from others. 

## Examples
- 你听说他要辞职了吗？这只是道听途说。<br>Have you heard he is quitting? That’s just hearsay.
- 不要相信道听途说，最好自己去确认事实。<br>Don't believe hearsay; it's best to verify the facts yourself.
- 道听途说的消息常常不靠谱。<br>Information from hearsay is often unreliable.
