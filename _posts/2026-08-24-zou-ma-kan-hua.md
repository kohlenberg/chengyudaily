## Overview
To take a cursory glance; to have a superficial understanding.

## Phrase
**走马看花** — zǒu mǎ kàn huā
## Characters


走 (zǒu) — to walk  
马 (mǎ) — horse  
看 (kàn) — to look  
花 (huā) — flower


## Origin
In ancient China, there was a famous poet who would ride through gardens on horseback, admiring the flowers while barely stopping to appreciate them. His friends would joke that he never truly saw their beauty, as he was always in a hurry. Thus, this phrase emerged to describe someone who observes things without paying full attention.

## Examples
- 他走马看花，没注意到那幅画的细节。<br>He took a cursory glance and didn’t notice the details of the painting.
- 在会议上，他只是走马看花，没有真正理解我们的计划。<br>In the meeting, he just skimmed through and didn’t truly understand our plan.
- 如果你走马看花，怎么能学到真正的知识？<br>If you just take a quick glance, how can you learn true knowledge?
