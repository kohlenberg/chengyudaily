## Overview
Ride the wind and break the waves; to forge ahead despite difficulties.

## Phrase
**乘风破浪** — chéng fēng pò làng
## Characters


乘 (chéng) — to ride  
风 (fēng) — wind  
破 (pò) — to break  
浪 (làng) — waves


## Origin
This idiom originates from the maritime adventures of ancient Chinese sailors who faced turbulent waters. They needed to harness the wind to navigate and overcome challenges at sea. Success required bravery, skill, and a determination to push through obstacles, just like in life.

## Examples
- 他在事业上乘风破浪，取得了巨大的成功。<br>He has ridden the wind and broken the waves in his career, achieving great success.
- 面对困难，我们要乘风破浪，永不放弃。<br>In the face of difficulties, we must ride the wind and break the waves, never giving up.
- 她的勇气让她在这个竞争激烈的行业中乘风破浪。<br>Her courage allows her to ride the wind and break the waves in this fiercely competitive industry.

## Closing
乘风破浪 means to ride the wind and break the waves; it’s about pushing forward despite challenges.
