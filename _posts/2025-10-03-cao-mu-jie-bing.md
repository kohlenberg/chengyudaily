> Today, we explore a phrase that captures the essence of paranoia and fear.

## Overview
Every blade of grass and every tree is a soldier; a state of extreme anxiety.

## Phrase
**草木皆兵** — cǎo mù jiē bīng
## Characters


草 (cǎo) — grass  
木 (mù) — tree  
皆 (jiē) — all  
兵 (bīng) — soldiers


## Origin
The idiom originated from a historical account during the Warring States period. Soldiers were so paranoid that they believed the rustling of grass and trees was an attack from the enemy. This heightened fear caused them to see danger in every shadow. Over time, this phrase came to symbolize a state of heightened anxiety in general.

## Examples
- 他最近感到草木皆兵，连朋友的电话也让他紧张。<br>He has been feeling like every blade of grass is a soldier, even his friends' calls make him anxious.
- 在战争期间，士兵们草木皆兵，随时准备战斗。<br>During the war, the soldiers were on edge, ready for battle at the slightest sound.
- 由于他的失误，整个团队草木皆兵，害怕出错。<br>Due to his mistake, the whole team was in a state of paranoia, afraid to make a mistake.
