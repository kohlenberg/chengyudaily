## Overview
Invite a wolf into the house; figuratively, to invite trouble.

## Phrase
**引狼入室** — yǐn láng rù shì
## Characters


引 (yǐn) — to lead or to pull  
狼 (láng) — wolf  
入 (rù) — to enter  
室 (shì) — room or house


## Origin
This idiom comes from ancient times when there was a story about a man who, despite knowing the dangers of a wolf, decided to bring one into his home as a pet. Predictably, the wolf caused havoc, illustrating the perils of making dangerous choices. This story serves as a cautionary tale against letting in threats that may seem harmless at first.

## Examples
- 他引狼入室，结果失去了所有的财产。<br>He invited trouble and ended up losing all his possessions.
- 你不能引狼入室，信任那些不可靠的人。<br>You can’t invite danger by trusting unreliable people.
- 这个项目听起来不错，但小心引狼入室。<br>This project sounds good, but be careful not to invite trouble.
