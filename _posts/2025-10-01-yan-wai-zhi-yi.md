## Overview
literal meaning: meaning beyond words; figurative meaning: implied meaning.

## Phrase
**言外之意** — yán wài zhī yì
## Characters


言 (yán) — speech, words  
外 (wài) — outside, beyond  
之 (zhī) — of, 's  
意 (yì) — meaning, intention


## Origin
This idiom originates from ancient Chinese literature, where subtle hints and unspoken thoughts were as important as spoken words. Poets and philosophers often relied on the context and nuances in their writings to convey deeper meanings. The ability to understand these implied messages was a mark of intelligence and insight. Thus, the phrase evolved to highlight the importance of what is not directly said.

## Examples
- 他说得很简单，但言外之意是他不想来。<br>He said it simply, but the implication is that he doesn't want to come.
- 从他的表情中，我能感受到言外之意。<br>From his expression, I can sense the implied meaning.
- 这句话的言外之意让人深思。<br>The implied meaning of this sentence is thought-provoking.
