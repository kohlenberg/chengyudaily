Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever heard someone worry about the sky falling? Let’s dive into that idea with today’s idiom. The phrase is: 杞人忧天, qǐ rén yōu tiān.

## Overview
Worried about something that is unlikely to happen.

## Phrase
**杞人忧天** — qǐ rén yōu tiān
## Characters


杞 (qǐ) — a state in ancient China  
人 (rén) — person  
忧 (yōu) — worry  
天 (tiān) — sky or heaven


## Origin
This idiom originates from an ancient tale about a man from the state of Qi. He was so worried that the sky would fall that he couldn’t focus on anything else. His anxiety became a source of ridicule among others, who pointed out that such a fear was irrational and unfounded. Still, it highlights how some people can become consumed by worries that are beyond reality.

## Examples
- 他总是杞人忧天，担心工作会丢。<br>He always worries unnecessarily, fearing he might lose his job.
- 不要杞人忧天，明天的事情等明天再说。<br>Don’t worry about things that might not happen; we’ll deal with tomorrow when it comes.
- 她对未来的担忧就像杞人忧天。<br>Her worries about the future are just like that of the man from Qi.

## Closing
So, 杞人忧天 means to worry about something that is unlikely to happen. Thanks for listening to Chengyu Bites! See you next time for another idiom.
