## Overview
To observe things superficially; to get a quick glance.

## Phrase
**走马观花** — zǒu mǎ guān huā
## Characters


走 (zǒu) — to walk or to go  
马 (mǎ) — horse  
观 (guān) — to observe or to view  
花 (huā) — flower


## Origin
This idiom originated from the experience of travelers who would ride horses past blooming flowers, unable to appreciate their beauty in detail. They could only catch a glimpse as they hurried by, which illustrates how we often miss the deeper essence of things when we rush through life.

## Examples
- 他总是走马观花，看书的时候不认真。<br>He always skims through books without paying attention.
- 在这个快节奏的社会，我们常常走马观花，忽视了生活的细节。<br>In this fast-paced society, we often observe things superficially and overlook the details of life.
- 旅行的时候，别只走马观花，要好好体验每个地方。<br>When traveling, don’t just take a quick look; really experience each place.
