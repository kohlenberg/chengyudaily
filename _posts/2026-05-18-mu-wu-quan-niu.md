## Overview
To see the whole cow; to focus so intently that other details are ignored.

## Phrase
**目无全牛** — mù wú quán niú
## Characters

目 (mù) — eye  
无 (wú) — without  
全 (quán) — whole  
牛 (niú) — cow


## Origin
This idiom comes from a story about a skilled butcher who was so proficient at his craft that he could cut up an entire cow without needing to pay attention to its individual parts. He had such a deep understanding of the cow's structure that he could see it as a whole rather than just a collection of parts. His mastery allowed him to work efficiently and effectively without distraction.

## Examples
- 他在工作时目无全牛，完全忘了吃饭。<br>He was so focused on work that he completely forgot to eat.
- 她讨论问题时目无全牛，忽略了其他重要的观点。<br>She was so focused on her point that she overlooked other important perspectives.
- 学生们在考试时，有些目无全牛，导致了失误。<br>Some students were so focused during the exam that they made mistakes.

## Closing
Remember the idiom: 目无全牛, which means to focus so intently that other details are ignored.
