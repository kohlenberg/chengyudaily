## Overview
A castle in the air; unrealistic or impractical dreams.

## Phrase
**空中楼阁** — kōng zhōng lóu gé
## Characters


空 (kōng) — empty or void  
中 (zhōng) — in or among  
楼 (lóu) — building or floor  
阁 (gé) — pavilion or loft


## Origin
This idiom originates from ancient Chinese literature, where it depicted grand, imaginary structures that existed only in one's mind. These were often used to symbolize aspirations that are beautiful but unattainable. Just like a castle in the air, they can seem inviting but lack a solid foundation. Thus, it serves as a caution against getting lost in unrealistic fantasies.

## Examples
- 他总是谈论他的空中楼阁。<br>He always talks about his castles in the air.
- 不要沉迷于空中楼阁，现实更重要。<br>Don’t get lost in unrealistic dreams; reality is more important.
- 这个计划就是个空中楼阁。<br>This plan is just a castle in the air.
