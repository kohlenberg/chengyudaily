Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever wondered how a simple musical term became a warning against deception? [break 1s] The phrase is: 滥竽充数, làn yú chōng shù. [break 1s] 滥 (làn) means 'to mix' or 'to flood' [break 0.5s] 竽 (yú) refers to a type of musical instrument, specifically a bamboo reed instrument [break 0.5s] 充 (chōng) means 'to fill' [break 0.5s] 数 (shù) means 'number' or 'to count'. [break 1s] So together, 滥竽充数 literally means 'filling the number with mixed instruments', and figuratively, it refers to someone who pretends to be competent but is actually unqualified. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] In ancient China, there was a king who loved music. He hired a group of musicians to play for him, including a man who could not actually play the instrument he pretended to know, the 笙 (shēng). He simply blended in with the others, making it hard to identify his lack of skill. Eventually, the king discovered the truth and realized that not everyone in his court was genuinely talented. [break 1.5s] Example 1: 他滥竽充数，根本不会做这个工作。 [break 1s] He fills the ranks with inferior quality; he can't do this job at all. [break 1s] Example 2: 别让那些滥竽充数的人进公司。 [break 1s] Don't let those unqualified people into the company. [break 1s] Example 3: 我们需要真正的专家，而不是滥竽充数的人。 [break 1s] We need real experts, not impostors. [break 1s] So, that's 滥竽充数, meaning filling the ranks with inferior quality. Thank you for listening to Chengyu Bites! See you next time for another idiom. [break 1s]
