Today, we dive into a phrase that describes situations beyond repair.[break 1s] The phrase is: 不可救药, bù kě jiù yào.[break 0.5s] 不 (bù) - no; not[break 0.5s] 可 (kě) - can; able[break 0.5s] 救 (jiù) - rescue; save[break 0.5s] 药 (yào) - medicine; remedy[break 0.5s] Full idiom: 不可救药 literally means 'cannot be saved by medicine' and figuratively means 'beyond remedy' or 'hopeless'.[break 1s] Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s] This idiom originates from ancient Chinese literature, where it was said that certain ailments could not be cured by medicine. Over time, it evolved to describe situations that are irreparable, whether in personal life or broader contexts. The idea is that once something is beyond help, no remedy can fix it. It serves as a cautionary reminder of the limits of intervention.[break 1.5s] Example 1: 他的习惯已经不可救药了。  
His habits are already beyond remedy.[break 1s] Example 2: 这个项目的损失不可救药。
