Let’s dive into that idea with today’s idiom.[break 1s]The phrase is: 杞人忧天, qǐ rén yōu tiān.[break 0.5s]杞 (qǐ) - a state in ancient China, [break 0.5s]人 (rén) - person, [break 0.5s]忧 (yōu) - worry, [break 0.5s]天 (tiān) - sky or heaven.[break 1s]So, 杞人忧天 literally means 'the person from Qi worries about the sky,' which figuratively means to worry about something unlikely to happen.[break 1s]Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s]This idiom originates from an ancient tale about a man from the state of Qi. He was so worried that the sky would fall that he couldn’t focus on anything else. His anxiety became a source of ridicule among others, who pointed out that such a fear was irrational and unfounded. Still, it highlights how some people can become consumed by worries that are beyond reality.[break 1.5s]Example 1: 他总是杞人忧天，担心工作会丢。  [break 1s]He always worries unnecessarily, fearing he might lose his job.[break 1s]Example 2: 不要杞人忧天，明天的事情等明天再说。  [break 1s]Don’t worry about things that might not happen; we’ll deal with tomorrow when it comes.[break 1s]Example 3: 她对未来的担忧就像杞人忧天。  [break 1s]Her worries about the future are just like that of the man from Qi.[break 1s]So, 杞人忧天 means to worry about something that is unlikely to happen.
