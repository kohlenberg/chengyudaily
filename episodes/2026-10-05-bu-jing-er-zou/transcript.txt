This idiom captures that perfectly.[break 1s]The phrase is: 不胫而走, bù jìng ér zǒu.[break 0.5s]不 (bù) - not[break 0.5s]胫 (jìng) - shank or leg[break 0.5s]而 (ér) - and[break 0.5s]走 (zǒu) - to walk or run[break 1s]So, 不胫而走 literally means 'to run without legs' and figuratively refers to something that spreads rapidly and widely.[break 1s]Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s]This idiom originates from an ancient tale where information spread like a rumor without anyone actually seeing it travel. It highlights how quickly news can travel, much like a person running without needing legs. This vivid imagery helps convey the idea of fast dissemination.[break 1.5s]Example 1: 这个消息不胫而走，大家都知道了。  This news spread rapidly, and everyone knows about it.[break 1s]Example 2: 她的成功故事在学校里不胫而走。 Her success story spread quickly throughout the school.[break 1s]Example 3: 这个谣言不胫而走，让人感到惊讶。 This rumor spread quickly, surprising everyone.[break 1s]So, 不胫而走 means to spread rapidly without legs; thank you for listening!
