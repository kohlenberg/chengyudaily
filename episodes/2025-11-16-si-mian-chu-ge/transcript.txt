Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. This idiom describes a situation where one feels trapped and outnumbered. [break 1s] The phrase is: 四面楚歌, sì miàn chǔ gē. [break 1s] 四 (sì) - four [break 0.5s] 面 (miàn) - sides [break 0.5s] 楚 (chǔ) - Chu (a historical state) [break 0.5s] 歌 (gē) - song [break 1s] So, 四面楚歌 literally means 'four sides, Chu songs' and figuratively refers to being surrounded by enemies and feeling isolated. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originates from a historical event during the Qin dynasty when the army of Xiang Yu was encircled by the enemy. As they were outnumbered and in despair, they heard songs from the Chu region sung by the enemy soldiers, which reminded them of their homeland and their hopeless situation. This led to the phrase becoming synonymous with feeling trapped and desolate. [break 1.5s] Example 1: 他现在的处境就像四面楚歌。 [break 1s] His current situation is like being surrounded on all sides by enemies. [break 1s] Example 2: 在会议上，她感到四面楚歌，无法表达自己的意见。 [break 1s] At the meeting, she felt surrounded and unable to express her opinion. [break 1s] Example 3: 面对竞争对手，他觉得四面楚歌，压力很大。 [break 1s] Facing his competitors, he feels trapped and under a lot of pressure. [break 1s] So, 四面楚歌 means feeling surrounded and isolated.
