Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever tried to find something in the dark? This idiom captures that feeling perfectly. [break 1s] The phrase is: 瞎子摸鱼 (xiā zi mō yú) [break 1s] 瞎 (xiā) - blind [break 0.5s] 子 (zi) - person [break 0.5s] 摸 (mō) - to feel or touch [break 0.5s] 鱼 (yú) - fish [break 0.5s] The full idiom again: 瞎子摸鱼 means 'blind man feeling for fish', which figuratively describes making guesses without proper knowledge. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originates from a time when people believed that a blind man might try to catch fish by simply feeling around in the water. Without sight, his chances of success were slim, symbolizing the futility of attempting something without the necessary skills or understanding. It reflects a humorous yet sobering reality about blindly trying to achieve a goal. [break 1.5s] Example one: 他做这个项目就像瞎子摸鱼。 [break 1s] He is working on this project like a blind man feeling for fish. [break 1s] Example two: 没有准备就参与比赛，简直是瞎子摸鱼。 [break 1s] Joining the competition without preparation is just like a blind man feeling for fish. [break 1s] Example three: 在不懂的情况下做决定，就像瞎子摸鱼。 [break 1s] Making decisions without understanding is like a blind man feeling for fish.
