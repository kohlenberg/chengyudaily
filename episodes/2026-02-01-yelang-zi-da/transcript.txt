Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever heard of someone who thinks they are the best without realizing their limitations? [break 1s] The phrase is: 夜郎自大, Yèláng zì dà. [break 0.5s] 夜 (Yè) - night [break 0.5s] 郎 (láng) - a young man or a person [break 0.5s] 自 (zì) - self [break 0.5s] 大 (dà) - big or great [break 1s] So, the full idiom is 夜郎自大, which literally means 'the night man thinks he is great' and figuratively refers to being blindly arrogant or overestimating oneself. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] The idiom originates from a historical account about a small kingdom called Yelang, which was located in what is now southwestern China. The king of Yelang, believing his kingdom was the most powerful, refused to acknowledge the existence of larger and more influential states around him. This arrogance eventually led to the downfall of his kingdom when stronger forces challenged him. [break 1.5s] Example 1: 他总是认为自己是公司里最聪明的，真是夜郎自大。 [break 1s] He always thinks he is the smartest in the company; he is truly blindly arrogant. [break 1s] Example 2: 她在比赛中表现得夜郎自大，结果输了。 [break 1s] She acted arrogantly in the competition and ended up losing. [break 1s] Example 3: 有些人对自己的能力夜郎自大，结果在工作中犯了很多错误。 [break 1s] Some people are blindly arrogant about their abilities and end up making many mistakes at work. [break 1s] So, remember the idiom 夜郎自大, which means to be blindly arrogant or overestimate oneself. Thank you for listening to Chengyu Bites! See you next time for another idiom. [break 1s]
