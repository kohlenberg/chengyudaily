Today, we explore a phrase that reflects the shared grief of friends in peril.[break 1s] The phrase is: 兔死狐悲, tù sǐ hú bēi.[break 0.5s] 兔 (tù) means 'rabbit'[break 0.5s] 死 (sǐ) means 'to die'[break 0.5s] 狐 (hú) means 'fox'[break 0.5s] 悲 (bēi) means 'to grieve'.[break 1s] The full idiom is 兔死狐悲, which literally means 'the death of a rabbit causes sorrow for a fox.' Figuratively, it expresses the idea of feeling sorrow for someone else's misfortune, especially when it reflects our own vulnerabilities.[break 1s] Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s] In ancient times, a fox and a rabbit were close friends. One day, the rabbit fell into a trap and died. The fox grieved deeply, realizing that the rabbit's death was a warning of the dangers that could also befall him. This idiom emerged from that poignant moment, symbolizing the shared grief amongst friends facing similar threats.[break 1.5s] Example one: 朋友的失业让我感到兔死狐悲。  My friend's unemployment makes me feel sorrowful about our shared struggles.[break 1s] Example two: 当我听到邻居的灾难，我感到兔死狐悲。 When I heard about my neighbor's disaster, I felt a sense of shared grief.[break 1s] Example three: 看到同事离职，我心中充满兔死狐悲。 Seeing a colleague leave filled me with sorrow for our shared experiences.[break 1s] So, remember the idiom 兔死狐悲, which means to feel sorrow for someone else's misfortune.
