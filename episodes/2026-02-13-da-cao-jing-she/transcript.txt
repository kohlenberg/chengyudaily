Let's dive in![break 1s]The phrase is: 打草惊蛇 (dǎ cǎo jīng shé)[break 0.5s]打 (dǎ) - to hit[break 0.5s]草 (cǎo) - grass[break 0.5s]惊 (jīng) - to scare[break 0.5s]蛇 (shé) - snake[break 1s]So,打草惊蛇 literally means 'to hit the grass and scare the snake.' Figuratively, it refers to actions that inadvertently alert others to your intentions or plans.[break 1s]Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s]In ancient times, a hunter was trying to catch a snake hiding in the grass. He started hitting the grass, thinking it would flush the snake out. Instead, the noise scared the snake away, teaching him that sometimes, revealing your plans can lead to failure. This led to the saying that emphasizes caution in how one approaches a situation.[break 1.5s]Example 1: 他打草惊蛇，结果计划失败了。[break 1s]He stirred up trouble and ended up failing his plan.[break 1s]Example 2: 你不应该告诉太多人，这样会打草惊蛇。[break 1s]You shouldn't tell too many people; it will alert the enemy.[break 1s]Example 3: 打草惊蛇之后，敌人开始警觉。[break 1s]After stirring up trouble, the enemy became alert.[break 1s]So, 打草惊蛇 means to stir up trouble and alert the enemy.
