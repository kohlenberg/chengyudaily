Ever wondered how lofty dreams can sometimes be just illusions?[break 1s]The phrase is: 空中楼阁, kōng zhōng lóu gé.[break 0.5s]空 (kōng) - empty or void[break 0.5s]中 (zhōng) - in or among[break 0.5s]楼 (lóu) - building or floor[break 0.5s]阁 (gé) - pavilion or loft[break 1s]So, 空中楼阁 literally means 'an empty building in the air' and figuratively refers to unrealistic dreams or plans.[break 1s]Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s]This idiom originates from ancient Chinese literature, where it depicted grand, imaginary structures that existed only in one's mind. These were often used to symbolize aspirations that are beautiful but unattainable. Just like a castle in the air, they can seem inviting but lack a solid foundation. Thus, it serves as a caution against getting lost in unrealistic fantasies.[break 1.5s]Example 1: 他总是谈论他的空中楼阁。  
He always talks about his castles in the air.[break 1s]Example 2: 不要沉迷于空中楼阁，现实更重要。  
Don’t get lost in unrealistic dreams; reality is more important.[break 1s]Example 3: 这个计划就是个空中楼阁。  
This plan is just a castle in the air.[break 1s]So, 空中楼阁 means a castle in the air, symbolizing unrealistic or impractical dreams. Thank you for listening to Chengyu Bites! See you next time for another idiom.[break 1s]
