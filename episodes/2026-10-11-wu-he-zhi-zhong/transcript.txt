Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever wondered what it means when a group lacks unity and purpose? [break 1s] The phrase is: 乌合之众, wū hé zhī zhòng. [break 0.5s] 乌 (wū) means 'crow' [break 0.5s] 合 (hé) means 'to gather' [break 0.5s] 之 (zhī) is a possessive particle, often meaning 'of' [break 0.5s] 众 (zhòng) means 'crowd' or 'multitude'. [break 1s] So, when we put it all together, we have 乌合之众, which literally translates to 'a crowd of crows' and figuratively refers to a disorganized or chaotic group. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] The idiom originates from a historical context where a disorganized group of people, much like crows that gather without purpose, was used to describe an unruly mob. This imagery emphasizes the lack of cohesion and effectiveness within such a group. Over time, it has come to represent any assembly of people that lacks discipline or organization. [break 1.5s] Example 1: 这场示威活动完全是乌合之众。 [break 1s] This protest is completely a disorganized crowd. [break 1s] Example 2: 在没有领导的情况下，他们成了乌合之众。 [break 1s] Without leadership, they became a rabble. [break 1s] Example 3: 面对危机，乌合之众不能解决问题。 [break 1s] In the face of a crisis, a disorganized crowd can’t solve the problem. [break 1s] So, that's 乌合之众, meaning a disorganized crowd.
