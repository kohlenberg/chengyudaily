Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever wondered how a quick glance can lead to misunderstandings? [break 1s] The phrase is: 走马看花, zǒu mǎ kàn huā. [break 1s] 走 (zǒu) - to walk [break 0.5s] 马 (mǎ) - horse [break 0.5s] 看 (kàn) - to look [break 0.5s] 花 (huā) - flower [break 1s] The full idiom again: 走马看花 means to ride a horse while looking at flowers. Literally, it describes a quick and superficial view of something beautiful, figuratively implying a hasty understanding without depth. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] In ancient China, there was a famous poet who would ride through gardens on horseback, admiring the flowers while barely stopping to appreciate them. His friends would joke that he never truly saw their beauty, as he was always in a hurry. Thus, this phrase emerged to describe someone who observes things without paying full attention. [break 1.5s] Example one: 他走马看花，没注意到那幅画的细节。 [break 1s] He took a cursory glance and didn’t notice the details of the painting. [break 1s] Example two: 在会议上，他只是走马看花，没有真正理解我们的计划。 [break 1s] In the meeting, he just skimmed through and didn’t truly understand our plan. [break 1s] Example three: 如果你走马看花，怎么能学到真正的知识？ [break 1s] If you just take a quick glance, how can you learn true knowledge? [break 1s] So, remember, 走马看花 means to take a cursory glance; don’t rush through life! Thanks for listening to Chengyu Bites! See you next time for another idiom. [break 1s]
