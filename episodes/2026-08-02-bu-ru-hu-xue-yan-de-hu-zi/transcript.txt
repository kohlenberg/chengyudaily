Today, we explore the idea of bravery and reward in Chinese culture.[break 1s] The phrase is: 不入虎穴，焉得虎子, bù rù hǔ xué, yān dé hǔ zǐ.[break 0.5s] 不 (bù) - not[break 0.5s] 入 (rù) - enter[break 0.5s] 虎 (hǔ) - tiger[break 0.5s] 穴 (xué) - den[break 0.5s] ， (,) - [pause] 焉 (yān) - how[break 0.5s] 得 (dé) - can/get[break 0.5s] 虎 (hǔ) - tiger[break 0.5s] 子 (zǐ) - cub[break 1s] The full idiom is 不入虎穴，焉得虎子. Literally, it means 'If you don't enter the tiger's den, how can you get the tiger's cub?' Figuratively, it conveys that you must take risks to achieve great rewards.[break 1s] Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s] This idiom originates from a tale about a brave hunter who wanted to catch tiger cubs. He realized that to succeed, he needed to enter the tiger's den, which was fraught with danger. The story teaches us that without facing challenges, we cannot hope to gain valuable rewards. It's a reminder of the bravery required to achieve great things.[break 1.5s] Example one: 不入虎穴，焉得虎子，只有勇敢的人才能成功。 [break 1s] You must take risks to achieve success; only brave people can succeed. [break 1s] Example two: 如果你想创业，必须冒险，不入虎穴，焉得虎子。 [break 1s] If you want to start a business, you must take risks; you can't achieve rewards without taking chances. [break 1s] Example three: 她总是说，不入虎穴，焉得虎子，这激励了我去追求梦想。 [break 1s] She always says, 'You must take risks to achieve great rewards,' which inspires me to pursue my dreams.
