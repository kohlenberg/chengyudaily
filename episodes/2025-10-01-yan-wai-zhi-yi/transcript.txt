Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Today, we dive into the world of implied meanings. [break 1s] The phrase is: 言外之意, yán wài zhī yì. [break 1s] 言 (yán) - speech, words. [break 0.5s] 外 (wài) - outside, beyond. [break 0.5s] 之 (zhī) - of, 's. [break 0.5s] 意 (yì) - meaning, intention. [break 1s] The full idiom is 言外之意, which literally means 'meaning beyond words' and figuratively represents an implied meaning. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originates from ancient Chinese literature, where subtle hints and unspoken thoughts were as important as spoken words. Poets and philosophers often relied on the context and nuances in their writings to convey deeper meanings. The ability to understand these implied messages was a mark of intelligence and insight. Thus, the phrase evolved to highlight the importance of what is not directly said. [break 1.5s] Example 1: 他说得很简单，但言外之意是他不想来。 [break 1s] He said it simply, but the implication is that he doesn't want to come. [break 1s] Example 2: 从他的表情中，我能感受到言外之意。 [break 1s] From his expression, I can sense the implied meaning. [break 1s] Example 3: 这句话的言外之意让人深思。 [break 1s] The implied meaning of this sentence is thought-provoking. [break 1s] So, today’s idiom was 言外之意, meaning implied meaning.
