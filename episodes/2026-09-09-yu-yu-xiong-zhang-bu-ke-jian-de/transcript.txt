This idiom captures that dilemma perfectly.[break 1s]The phrase is: 鱼与熊掌不可兼得, yú yǔ xióng zhǎng bù kě jiān dé.[break 0.5s]鱼 (yú) means 'fish'[break 0.5s]与 (yǔ) means 'and'[break 0.5s]熊 (xióng) means 'bear'[break 0.5s]掌 (zhǎng) means 'paw'[break 0.5s]不 (bù) means 'not'[break 0.5s]可 (kě) means 'can'[break 0.5s]兼得 (jiān dé) means 'have both'.[break 1s]So, the full idiom means 鱼与熊掌不可兼得, literally 'You cannot have both fish and bear's paw', which figuratively means you have to make a choice between two desirable options.[break 1s]Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s]This idiom originates from a fable about a person who had to choose between enjoying a delicious fish or a bear's paw, which was considered a delicacy. It illustrates the idea that sometimes, in life, we must make tough decisions and cannot have everything we want. The phrase serves as a reminder of the importance of prioritizing what truly matters to us.[break 1.5s]Example 1: 你想去旅行，但也想存钱，鱼与熊掌不可兼得。[break 1s]You want to travel, but you also want to save money; you can't have both. [break 1s]Example 2: 在工作和休息之间，你必须选择，鱼与熊掌不可兼得。[break 1s]Between work and rest, you must choose; you can't have both. [break 1s]Example 3: 如果你想追求梦想，就得放弃一些舒适，鱼与熊掌不可兼得。[break 1s]If you want to pursue your dreams, you have to give up some comfort; you can't have both.
