Today, we uncover how a glimpse can lead to misunderstanding.[break 1s]The phrase is: 窥豹一斑 (kuī bào yī bān).[break 0.5s]窥 (kuī) - to peek;[break 0.5s]豹 (bào) - leopard;[break 0.5s]一 (yī) - one;[break 0.5s]斑 (bān) - spot or mark.[break 1s]So, 窥豹一斑 literally means 'to peek at a leopard and see only one spot.' Figuratively, it refers to having a limited understanding of a larger situation.[break 1s]Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s]In ancient China, there was a story about a person who saw only a part of a leopard—just one of its spots. This person thought they understood the whole animal based on that limited view. The saying evolved to remind people that seeing only a small part can lead to misconceptions about the whole picture.[break 1.5s]Example 1: 他只看了报告的一部分，就认为这个项目失败了。  
He only looked at part of the report and thought the project had failed.[break 1s]Example 2: 不要只听一面之词，应该全面了解情况。  
Don't just listen to one side; you should understand the situation comprehensively.[break 1s]Example 3: 我们不能仅凭这一点信息就做出决定。  
We can't make a decision based on just this piece of information.[break 1s]So, 窥豹一斑 means seeing only a part of something and having a limited understanding. Thanks for listening to Chengyu Bites! See you next time for another idiom.[break 1s]
