Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever wondered how a cornered dog might react? Let's find out! [break 1s] The phrase is: 狗急跳墙, gǒu jí tiàoqiáng. [break 1s] 狗 (gǒu) means 'dog' [break 0.5s] 急 (jí) means 'urgent' or 'desperate' [break 0.5s] 跳 (tiào) means 'to jump' [break 0.5s] 墙 (qiáng) means 'wall.' [break 1s] So, the full idiom is 狗急跳墙, which literally means 'a dog in desperation jumps over the wall' and figuratively means 'a desperate person will take drastic actions.' [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] The idiom originates from an ancient tale where a dog, cornered by a hunter, jumped over a wall to escape. This act of desperation symbolizes how one might behave when faced with dire circumstances. It teaches us that urgency can lead to unexpected actions. [break 1.5s] Example one: 他被老板骂得很惨，狗急跳墙，决定辞职。 [break 1s] He was scolded harshly by his boss, and in desperation, he decided to resign. [break 1s] Example two: 她的存款快用光了，狗急跳墙，开始借钱。 [break 1s] Her savings were running low, and in desperation, she started borrowing money. [break 1s] Example three: 面对竞争对手，他狗急跳墙，试图压低价格。 [break 1s] Faced with competitors, he took drastic actions, trying to lower prices. [break 1s] So, remember, 狗急跳墙 means a desperate person will take drastic actions. Thank you for listening to Chengyu Bites! See you next time for another idiom. [break 1s]
