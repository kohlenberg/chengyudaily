Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Today, we explore a phrase that captures the essence of beauty in nature and beyond. [break 1s] The phrase is: 闭月羞花, bì yuè xiū huā. [break 0.5s] 闭 (bì) - to close, [break 0.5s] 月 (yuè) - moon, [break 0.5s] 羞 (xiū) - to shame, [break 0.5s] 花 (huā) - flower. [break 1s] The full idiom is 闭月羞花, which literally means 'to close the moon and shame the flowers,' and figuratively describes a woman's beauty that surpasses nature. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originates from ancient Chinese literature, where legends often depicted beautiful women whose beauty was so stunning that it could overshadow the moon and make flowers feel ashamed of their own appearance. One famous example is the beauty of the legendary figure Xi Shi, known for her mesmerizing looks. Her beauty was said to be so extraordinary that even nature seemed to pale in comparison. [break 1.5s] Example one: 她的美丽让人闭月羞花。 [break 1s] Her beauty makes the moon close and the flowers ashamed. [break 1s] Example two: 这位舞者真是闭月羞花。 [break 1s] This dancer is truly stunning, surpassing even the beauty of nature. [break 1s] Example three: 看到她，我觉得自己也闭月羞花。 [break 1s] Seeing her, I feel like I am outshone by her beauty. [break 1s] So, remember the idiom 闭月羞花, which means a beauty that surpasses nature.
