Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Have you ever waited for luck to strike without taking action? [break 1s] The phrase is: 守株待兔, shǒu zhū dài tù. [break 1s] 守 (shǒu) - to guard or keep [break 0.5s] 株 (zhū) - a tree stump [break 0.5s] 待 (dài) - to wait [break 0.5s] 兔 (tù) - rabbit [break 0.5s] Full idiom: 守株待兔 means literally 'to guard a tree stump and wait for rabbits,' figuratively referring to waiting idly for opportunities to come without effort. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] In ancient times, a farmer saw a rabbit accidentally kill itself by crashing into a tree stump. Excited by this stroke of luck, he decided to stop working in his fields and instead waited by the stump for more rabbits to come. Days passed, but no more rabbits appeared, and his fields became overgrown. This story serves as a warning against relying solely on luck. [break 1.5s] Example one: 他总是守株待兔，不愿意主动寻找工作。 [break 1s] He always waits idly for opportunities and is unwilling to actively search for a job. [break 1s] Example two: 这种守株待兔的态度不会带来成功。 [break 1s] This kind of idle waiting will not bring success. [break 1s] Example three: 如果你守株待兔，就会错过很多机会。 [break 1s] If you wait idly for luck, you will miss many opportunities. [break 1s] So, remember, 守株待兔 reminds us that relying on luck alone is not the way to achieve our goals. Thank you for listening to Chengyu Bites! See you next time for another idiom. [break 1s]
