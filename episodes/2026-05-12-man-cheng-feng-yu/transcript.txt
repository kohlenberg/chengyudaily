Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Today, we explore how a storm of rumors can fill an entire city. [break 1s] The phrase is: 满城风雨, mǎn chéng fēng yǔ. [break 0.5s] 满 (mǎn) means 'full' or 'filled'. [break 0.5s] 城 (chéng) means 'city' or 'town'. [break 0.5s] 风 (fēng) means 'wind'. [break 0.5s] 雨 (yǔ) means 'rain'. [break 1s] So, the full idiom is 满城风雨, which literally translates to 'a city filled with wind and rain'. Figuratively, it refers to a situation where rumors or gossip are rampant throughout a community. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originates from a historical event where a city was engulfed in chaos due to the spread of false information. As rumors circulated like a storm, the citizens were left in confusion and anxiety. The imagery of wind and rain vividly depicts the turmoil and unrest caused by these whispers. [break 1.5s] Example 1: 最近，关于他辞职的传闻满城风雨。 [break 1s] Recently, rumors about his resignation are filling the city. [break 1s] Example 2: 这件事让满城风雨，大家都在讨论。 [break 1s] This matter has caused a storm of gossip throughout the city; everyone is talking about it. [break 1s] Example 3: 她的秘密被泄露，满城风雨。 [break 1s] Her secret was leaked, and now the city is filled with rumors.
