Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Have you ever heard someone talk a big game without the skills to back it up? [break 1s] The phrase is: 纸上谈兵, zhǐ shàng tán bīng. [break 1s] 纸 (zhǐ) means 'paper'. [break 0.5s] 上 (shàng) means 'on' or 'above'. [break 0.5s] 谈 (tán) means 'to talk' or 'to discuss'. [break 0.5s] 兵 (bīng) means 'soldiers' or 'military'. [break 1s] So, 纸上谈兵 literally translates to 'talking about military tactics on paper,' which figuratively means discussing theories without any practical application. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originates from the historical context of the Warring States period, where a general named Zhao Kuo was known for his theoretical knowledge of warfare. However, when he faced a real battle, his lack of practical experience led to his defeat. His reliance on strategy without hands-on skill became a cautionary tale. [break 1.5s] Example one: 他的计划太理想化了，完全是纸上谈兵。 [break 1s] His plan is too idealistic; it’s all just talk without action. [break 1s] Example two: 不要纸上谈兵，快去实践你的想法。 [break 1s] Don’t just talk about theories; go put your ideas into practice. [break 1s] Example three: 这位教授的理论很精彩，但在实际应用中仍然是纸上谈兵。 [break 1s] The professor's theories are brilliant, but they remain just talk when applied in practice. [break 1s] So, remember, 纸上谈兵 means to discuss ideas without practical experience. Thank you for listening to Chengyu Bites! See you next time for another idiom. [break 1s]
