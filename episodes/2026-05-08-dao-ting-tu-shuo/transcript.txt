Ever wondered how gossip spreads through whispers?[break 1s]The phrase is: 道听途说, dào tīng tú shuō.[break 0.5s]道 (dào) - way or path[break 0.5s]听 (tīng) - to hear[break 0.5s]途 (tú) - road or route[break 0.5s]说 (shuō) - to speak or say[break 1s]The idiom again is: 道听途说, which literally means 'to hear along the way and speak,' figuratively referring to hearsay or unverified information.[break 1s]Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s]This idiom originates from ancient times when travelers would share stories they heard during their journeys. Often, these tales were exaggerated or distorted, leading to misinformation. Thus, it cautions against believing everything you hear from others.[break 1.5s]Example 1: 你听说他要辞职了吗？这只是道听途说。[break 1s]Have you heard he is quitting? That’s just hearsay.[break 1s]Example 2: 不要相信道听途说，最好自己去确认事实。[break 1s]Don't believe hearsay; it's best to verify the facts yourself.[break 1s]Example 3: 道听途说的消息常常不靠谱。[break 1s]Information from hearsay is often unreliable.[break 1s]The idiom is 道听途说, meaning hearsay or rumors.
