Today, we're diving into a phrase that warns against fickleness.[break 1s] The phrase is: 朝三暮四, zhāo sān mù sì.[break 0.5s] 朝 (zhāo) - morning;[break 0.5s] 三 (sān) - three;[break 0.5s] 暮 (mù) - evening;[break 0.5s] 四 (sì) - four.[break 1s] So, 朝三暮四 literally means 'three in the morning, four in the evening', and it figuratively refers to someone who changes their mind often or is inconsistent.[break 1s] Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s] This idiom originates from a fable about a man who fed his monkeys. He promised them three nuts in the morning and four at night. When he changed his mind to four in the morning and three at night, the monkeys became upset. This illustrates how people can be easily dissatisfied with change, especially when it's unexpected.[break 1.5s] Example 1: 他总是朝三暮四，根本不可靠。[break 1s] He is always inconsistent; he is simply unreliable.[break 1s] Example 2: 政策的变化让大家朝三暮四。[break 1s] The changes in policy have made everyone inconsistent.[break 1s] Example 3: 她的态度朝三暮四，我不知道该相信什么。[break 1s] Her attitude is so fickle; I don’t know what to believe.[break 1s] So, 朝三暮四 means changing one's mind frequently; inconsistent.
