Today, we’re exploring a phrase that warns about letting danger too close.[break 1s] The phrase is: 引狼入室, yǐn láng rù shì.[break 0.5s] 引 (yǐn) means 'to lead' or 'to pull'.[break 0.5s] 狼 (láng) means 'wolf'.[break 0.5s] 入 (rù) means 'to enter'.[break 0.5s] 室 (shì) means 'room' or 'house'.[break 1s] So, it literally means 'lead a wolf into the house', which figuratively warns against inviting trouble or danger into your life.[break 1s] Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s] This idiom comes from ancient times when there was a story about a man who, despite knowing the dangers of a wolf, decided to bring one into his home as a pet. Predictably, the wolf caused havoc, illustrating the perils of making dangerous choices. This story serves as a cautionary tale against letting in threats that may seem harmless at first.[break 1.5s] Example 1: 他引狼入室，结果失去了所有的财产。 [break 1s] He invited trouble and ended up losing all his possessions. [break 1s] Example 2: 你不能引狼入室，信任那些不可靠的人。 [break 1s] You can’t invite danger by trusting unreliable people. [break 1s] Example 3: 这个项目听起来不错，但小心引狼入室。 [break 1s] This project sounds good, but be careful not to invite trouble. [break 1s] So, remember, 引狼入室 means to invite danger into your life.
