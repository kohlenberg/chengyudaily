[break 1s] 水 (shuǐ) means 'water.' [break 0.5s] 深 (shēn) means 'deep.' [break 0.5s] 火 (huǒ) means 'fire.' [break 0.5s] 热 (rè) means 'hot' or 'heat.' [break 1s] So, the full idiom 水深火热 literally translates to 'deep water and intense heat,' which figuratively means being in a difficult or suffering situation. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originates from ancient literature that describes the perilous conditions faced by those in war or disaster. It paints a picture of someone caught between the dangers of deep water and raging fire, emphasizing the dire straits one can find themselves in. Over time, it has come to symbolize any extreme hardship people endure in life.
