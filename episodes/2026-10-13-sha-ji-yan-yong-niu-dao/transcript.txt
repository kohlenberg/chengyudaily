Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever wondered how to express overkill in Chinese? This idiom has you covered! [break 1s] The phrase is: 杀鸡焉用牛刀, shā jī yān yòng niú dāo. [break 1s] 杀 (shā) - to kill [break 0.5s] 鸡 (jī) - chicken [break 0.5s] 焉 (yān) - why [break 0.5s] 用 (yòng) - to use [break 0.5s] 牛 (niú) - cow [break 0.5s] 刀 (dāo) - knife [break 1s] So, the full idiom is 杀鸡焉用牛刀, which literally means 'Why use a cow's knife to kill a chicken?' Figuratively, it means 'Using a big tool for a small task.' [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originates from ancient Chinese agricultural practices. Farmers used large knives to butcher bigger animals, but such tools were unnecessary for smaller tasks like killing a chicken. The saying emphasizes the absurdity of using overly complicated solutions for simple problems. Over time, it became a metaphor for wasting effort or resources on trivial matters. [break 1.5s] Example 1: 你用那么复杂的方法来解决这个问题，杀鸡焉用牛刀。 [break 1s] You're using such a complicated method to solve this problem; it's like using a big knife to kill a chicken. [break 1s] Example 2: 他花了几千块钱买工具来修电脑，真是杀鸡焉用牛刀。 [break 1s] He spent thousands on tools to fix the computer; that's really overkill. [break 1s] Example 3: 对于这个小项目，雇个专家简直是杀鸡焉用牛刀。 [break 1s] Hiring an expert for this small project is just overkill. [break 1s] So, remember, 杀鸡焉用牛刀 means using a big tool for a small task.
