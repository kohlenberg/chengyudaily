Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever start something with great enthusiasm only to let it fizzle out? This idiom captures that feeling.[break 1s]The phrase is: 虎头蛇尾, hǔ tóu shé wěi.[break 0.5s]虎 (hǔ) - tiger[break 0.5s]头 (tóu) - head[break 0.5s]蛇 (shé) - snake[break 0.5s]尾 (wěi) - tail[break 1s]So, 虎头蛇尾 literally means 'tiger head, snake tail'. Figuratively, it describes something that starts off strong but ends weakly.[break 1s]Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s]The idiom originates from a tale where a man carved a beautiful tiger's head but neglected to finish the tail, resulting in an incomplete work that looked odd. This illustrates how important it is to maintain consistency from beginning to end. The visual imagery of a powerful tiger head leading to a weak snake tail perfectly encapsulates this idea.[break 1.5s]Example 1: 他开始时很努力，但最后的报告却虎头蛇尾。[break 1s]He worked very hard at first, but the final report ended up being a weak finish. [break 1s]Example 2: 这个项目一开始很有潜力，结果却虎头蛇尾。[break 1s]This project had great potential at the beginning, but it ended up fizzling out. [break 1s]Example 3: 她的演讲虎头蛇尾，让人失望。[break 1s]Her speech started strong but ended up being disappointing. [break 1s]So, 虎头蛇尾 means a strong start with a weak finish.
