Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Today, we explore a phrase that captures the essence of paranoia and fear. [break 1s] The phrase is: 草木皆兵, cǎo mù jiē bīng. [break 1s] Let's break it down: [break 1s] 草 (cǎo) means 'grass' [break 0.5s] 木 (mù) means 'tree' [break 0.5s] 皆 (jiē) means 'all' [break 0.5s] 兵 (bīng) means 'soldiers' [break 1s] So, the full idiom, 草木皆兵, literally means 'Every blade of grass and every tree is a soldier.' Figuratively, it signifies a state of extreme anxiety where one sees threats everywhere. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] The idiom originated from a historical account during the Warring States period. Soldiers were so paranoid that they believed the rustling of grass and trees was an attack from the enemy. This heightened fear caused them to see danger in every shadow. Over time, this phrase came to symbolize a state of heightened anxiety in general. [break 1.5s] Here are three examples: [break 1s] 他最近感到草木皆兵，连朋友的电话也让他紧张。 He has been feeling like every blade of grass is a soldier, even his friends' calls make him anxious. [break 1s] 在战争期间，士兵们草木皆兵，随时准备战斗。 During the war, the soldiers were on edge, ready for battle at the slightest sound. [break 1s] 由于他的失误，整个团队草木皆兵，害怕出错。 Due to his mistake, the whole team was in a state of paranoia, afraid to make a mistake. [break 1s] So, remember, 草木皆兵 means a state of extreme anxiety.
