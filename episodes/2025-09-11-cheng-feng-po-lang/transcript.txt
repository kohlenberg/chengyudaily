[break 0.5s] 乘 (chéng) - to ride, [break 0.5s] 风 (fēng) - wind, [break 0.5s] 破 (pò) - to break, [break 0.5s] 浪 (làng) - waves. [break 1s] The full idiom again: 乘风破浪, literally meaning 'ride the wind and break the waves,' and figuratively encouraging us to forge ahead despite difficulties. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originates from the maritime adventures of ancient Chinese sailors who faced turbulent waters. They needed to harness the wind to navigate and overcome challenges at sea. Success required bravery, skill, and a determination to push through obstacles, just like in life. [break 1.5s] Here are three examples: [break 1s] 他在事业上乘风破浪，取得了巨大的成功。 He has ridden the wind and broken the waves in his career, achieving great success. [break 1s] 面对困难，我们要乘风破浪，永不放弃。 In the face of difficulties, we must ride the wind and break the waves, never giving up. [break 1s] 她的勇气让她在这个竞争激烈的行业中乘风破浪。 Her courage allows her to ride the wind and break the waves in this fiercely competitive industry.
