Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever wondered why some achievements come later in life? Let's dive into an inspiring idiom.[break 1s]The phrase is: 大器晚成 (dà qì wǎn chéng).[break 0.5s]大 (dà) means 'big' or 'great'[break 0.5s]器 (qì) means 'tool' or 'instrument'[break 0.5s]晚 (wǎn) means 'late'[break 0.5s]成 (chéng) means 'to become' or 'to succeed'[break 0.5s]So, it literally means 'a great tool becomes successful late.'[break 1s]Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s]This idiom originates from an ancient Chinese belief that just as a large tool takes time to forge, so too do great talents require time to mature. It reminds us that significant achievements often come after years of hard work and perseverance. Just like a fine wine, some qualities and skills get better with age. [break 1.5s]Example 1: 他虽然年轻，但我相信他会大器晚成。[break 1s]He is still young, but I believe he will achieve greatness in time.[break 1s]Example 2: 有些成功者都是大器晚成，经历了许多磨难。[break 1s]Many successful people are late bloomers, having gone through numerous hardships.[break 1s]Example 3: 不要着急，记住大器晚成的道理。[break 1s]Don't rush; remember the principle that great talents take time to develop.[break 1s]So, 大器晚成 means 'Great talents take time to develop.'
