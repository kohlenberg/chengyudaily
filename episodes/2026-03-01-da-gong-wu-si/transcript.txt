Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Today, we explore the essence of selflessness in leadership. [break 1s] The phrase is: 大公无私, dà gōng wú sī. [break 1s] 大 (dà) means 'big' or 'great'. [break 0.5s] 公 (gōng) means 'public' or 'fair'. [break 0.5s] 无 (wú) means 'without' or 'no'. [break 0.5s] 私 (sī) means 'self' or 'private'. [break 1s] So, together, 大公无私 literally means 'great public, no private', and figuratively, it signifies being impartial and selfless. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom stems from ancient Chinese governance, where leaders were expected to prioritize the welfare of the people over personal gain. It highlights the ideal conduct of officials who serve selflessly, ensuring fairness and justice. The concept is deeply ingrained in Confucian values, promoting the notion that true leadership is marked by public spirit. [break 1.5s] Example 1: 这个领导者真是大公无私。 [break 1s] This leader is truly selfless. [break 1s] Example 2: 在这个项目中，我们要大公无私，公平对待所有人。 [break 1s] In this project, we need to be impartial and treat everyone fairly. [break 1s] Example 3: 大公无私是优秀领导者必备的品质。 [break 1s] Selflessness is an essential quality of an excellent leader. [break 1s] So, that's 大公无私, meaning impartial and selfless.
