Have you ever wondered how actions can speak louder than words?[break 1s]The phrase is: 以身作则, yǐ shēn zuò zé.[break 0.5s]以 (yǐ) means 'to use'.[break 0.5s]身 (shēn) means 'body'.[break 0.5s]作 (zuò) means 'to do' or 'to make'.[break 0.5s]则 (zé) means 'rule' or 'standard'.[break 1s]So, the full idiom again: 以身作则 literally means 'use one's body to make a rule', which figuratively means to lead by example.[break 1s]Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s]This idiom originates from the idea that leaders must set a good example for their followers. It emphasizes the importance of demonstrating the behavior you wish to see in others. In ancient China, it was believed that a ruler's virtue would inspire the people to follow suit. Thus, leading by personal example became a key principle in governance and education.[break 1.5s]Example 1: 老师说，作为学生，我们应该以身作则。  
The teacher said that as students, we should lead by example.[break 1s]Example 2: 经理以身作则，大家都愿意跟随他的领导。  
The manager leads by example, and everyone is willing to follow his leadership.[break 1s]Example 3: 在家庭中，父母应以身作则，教导孩子。  
In a family, parents should lead by example in teaching their children.[break 1s]So, that’s 以身作则, which means to lead by example. Thank you for listening to Chengyu Bites! See you next time for another idiom.[break 1s]
