Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever wondered how hard lessons shape us into who we are? [break 1s] The phrase is: 不打不成器, bù dǎ bù chéng qì. [break 0.5s] 不 (bù) - no, not [break 0.5s] 打 (dǎ) - hit, strike [break 0.5s] 不 (bù) - no, not [break 0.5s] 成 (chéng) - become, turn into [break 0.5s] 器 (qì) - tool, device. [break 1s] The full idiom again: 不打不成器 literally means 'without hitting, you can't become a tool,' figuratively meaning 'no pain, no gain.' [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originates from the ancient practice of metalworking. Craftsmen would strike raw metal repeatedly to shape it into useful tools. Without this rigorous process, the metal would remain useless. This principle extends to life, suggesting that challenges and struggles are essential for personal growth and development. [break 1.5s] Example one: 你必须努力工作，不打不成器。 [break 1s] You must work hard; no pain, no gain. [break 1s] Example two: 学习新技能的时候，不打不成器。 [break 1s] When learning a new skill, no pain, no gain. [break 1s] Example three: 生活中有很多困难，但不打不成器。 [break 1s] There are many difficulties in life, but no pain, no gain. [break 1s] So, 不打不成器 reminds us that through challenges, we grow stronger.
