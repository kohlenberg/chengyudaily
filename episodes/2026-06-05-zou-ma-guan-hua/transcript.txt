Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever wondered how a horse ride can relate to a fleeting glance at beauty? [break 1s] The phrase is: 走马观花, zǒu mǎ guān huā. [break 0.5s] 走 (zǒu) means 'to walk' or 'to go', [break 0.5s] 马 (mǎ) means 'horse', [break 0.5s] 观 (guān) means 'to observe' or 'to view', [break 0.5s] 花 (huā) means 'flower'. [break 1s] So, 走马观花 literally means 'to observe flowers while riding a horse', which figuratively suggests seeing things only superficially. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originated from the experience of travelers who would ride horses past blooming flowers, unable to appreciate their beauty in detail. They could only catch a glimpse as they hurried by, which illustrates how we often miss the deeper essence of things when we rush through life. [break 1.5s] Example one: 他总是走马观花，看书的时候不认真。 [break 1s] He always skims through books without paying attention. [break 1s] Example two: 在这个快节奏的社会，我们常常走马观花，忽视了生活的细节。 [break 1s] In this fast-paced society, we often observe things superficially and overlook the details of life. [break 1s] Example three: 旅行的时候，别只走马观花，要好好体验每个地方。 [break 1s] When traveling, don’t just take a quick look; really experience each place. [break 1s] So, 走马观花 means to observe things superficially.
