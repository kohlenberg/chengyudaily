Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever wondered what a busy street in ancient China sounded like? [break 1s] The phrase is: 车水马龙, chē shuǐ mǎ lóng. [break 1s] 车 (chē) - car [break 0.5s] 水 (shuǐ) - water [break 0.5s] 马 (mǎ) - horse [break 0.5s] 龙 (lóng) - dragon [break 0.5s] The full idiom again: 车水马龙 literally means 'cars like flowing water and horses like dragons,' which figuratively describes heavy traffic or a bustling scene. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] This idiom originates from the busy streets of ancient China, where the sight of countless carriages and horses created a lively and chaotic atmosphere. It was said that the roads were so crowded that it resembled water flowing continuously. This vivid imagery has stuck around, symbolizing not just traffic, but also vibrant activity in any setting. [break 1.5s] Example one: 大街上车水马龙。 [break 1s] The main street is bustling with heavy traffic. [break 1s] Example two: 在节日的时候，市场总是车水马龙。 [break 1s] The market is always bustling during the festivals. [break 1s] Example three: 这座城市的夜生活真是车水马龙。 [break 1s] The nightlife in this city is truly vibrant and lively. [break 1s] So, we have 车水马龙, which means heavy traffic or a bustling scene.
