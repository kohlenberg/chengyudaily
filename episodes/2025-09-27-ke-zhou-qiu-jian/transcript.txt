Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever tried to find something using a method that just doesn't work? This idiom is for you.[break 1s]The phrase is: 刻舟求剑, kè zhōu qiú jiàn.[break 0.5s]刻 (kè) - to carve or engrave.[break 0.5s]舟 (zhōu) - boat.[break 0.5s]求 (qiú) - to seek or search for.[break 0.5s]剑 (jiàn) - sword.[break 1s]So, 刻舟求剑 literally means carving a mark on a boat to seek a sword, and figuratively, it refers to using an ineffective method to solve a problem.[break 1s]Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s]In ancient China, a man lost his sword while crossing a river. Instead of diving in to search for it, he marked the spot on the side of the boat where he dropped it. When the boat reached the shore, he thought he could find the sword by looking for the mark. This foolishness gave rise to the idiom, illustrating the absurdity of relying on outdated methods in changing circumstances.[break 1.5s]Example 1: 他刻舟求剑，根本没考虑到情况已经改变了。  He is carving a mark on the boat, not considering that the situation has changed.[break 1s]Example 2: 如果你继续刻舟求剑，你永远找不到解决方案。 If you keep seeking answers like this, you'll never find a solution.[break 1s]Example 3: 这种方法就像刻舟求剑，毫无意义。 This method is like carving a mark on a boat; it is meaningless.[break 1s]So, 刻舟求剑 means using an ineffective method to solve a problem.
