Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Today, we explore the limitations of our viewpoints. [break 1s] The phrase is: 坐井观天, zuò jǐng guān tiān. [break 1s] 坐 (zuò) means 'to sit' [break 0.5s] 井 (jǐng) means 'well' [break 0.5s] 观 (guān) means 'to observe' [break 0.5s] 天 (tiān) means 'sky'. [break 1s] So, 坐井观天 literally means 'to sit in a well and observe the sky', figuratively implying a limited perspective. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] Long ago, a frog lived in a deep well and believed that the sky was only the small circle above him. One day, a turtle from the sea visited and told him about the vastness of the world beyond his well. The frog was astonished but struggled to believe it. This story teaches us to recognize our limitations and seek broader perspectives. [break 1.5s] Example one: 他总是坐井观天，觉得自己知道的一切。 [break 1s] He always sees things narrowly, thinking he knows everything. [break 1s] Example two: 不要坐井观天，要多出去看看。 [break 1s] Don’t have a limited view; go out and see more. [break 1s] Example three: 他的观点太狭隘，真是坐井观天。 [break 1s] His viewpoint is too narrow; he truly sees things from a limited perspective. [break 1s] So, 坐井观天 means to have a limited perspective.
