Today, we explore a phrase that highlights the importance of interdependence.[break 1s] The phrase is: 唇亡齿寒, chún wáng chǐ hán.[break 0.5s] 唇, chún, means 'lips'[break 0.5s] 亡, wáng, means 'gone' or 'lost'[break 0.5s] 齿, chǐ, means 'teeth'[break 0.5s] 寒, hán, means 'cold'[break 1s] Together, it literally translates to 'When the lips are gone, the teeth feel cold,' and figuratively, it means that two parties are mutually dependent on each other.[break 1s] Here’s the story behind it:[break 1.5s] This idiom originates from a historical context where a conflict between two neighboring states led to one state losing its protective ally (the lips), resulting in vulnerability (the cold teeth) for the other. The loss of one party directly affected the well-being of the other, illustrating their interdependence.[break 1.5s] Example one: 朋友之间唇亡齿寒，互相帮助很重要。[break 1s] Among friends, when the lips are gone, the teeth feel cold; it’s important to help each other. [break 1s] Example two: 企业之间也唇亡齿寒，合作才能共赢。[break 1s] Between businesses, when the lips are gone, the teeth feel cold; cooperation leads to mutual success. [break 1s] Example three: 在国际关系中，唇亡齿寒的道理同样适用。[break 1s] In international relations, the principle of mutual dependence also applies. [break 1s] So today’s idiom is 唇亡齿寒, meaning mutual dependence.
