Welcome to Chengyu Bites — your quick summary on Chinese 成语. Ever wonder how to describe a chaotic mix of people? [break 1s] The phrase is: 鱼龙混杂, yú lóng hùn zá. [break 1s] 鱼 (yú) means 'fish' [break 0.5s] 龙 (lóng) means 'dragon' [break 0.5s] 混 (hùn) means 'to mix' [break 0.5s] 杂 (zá) means 'to be mixed' or 'mixed together' [break 1s] So, 鱼龙混杂 literally means 'fish and dragons mixed together'. Figuratively, it describes a situation where good and bad people are mixed together. [break 1s] Here’s the story behind it: [break 1.5s] In ancient times, the fish was seen as a common creature, while the dragon was a symbol of power and nobility. When these two were said to be mixed together, it indicated a confusing situation where high and low, good and bad, were all thrown into one place. This idiom reminds us to be cautious in our judgments, as appearances can be deceiving. [break 1.5s] 1) 这个市场里鱼龙混杂，买东西要小心。 [break 1s] This market is a mix of good and bad, so be careful when buying things. [break 1s] 2) 在这个社交圈，鱼龙混杂，难以分辨谁是真正的朋友。 [break 1s] In this social circle, it's hard to distinguish who your true friends are because of the mix of characters. [break 1s] 3) 他的演讲吸引了鱼龙混杂的听众。 [break 1s] His speech attracted a mixed audience of various types of people. [break 1s] So, 鱼龙混杂 means a mix of good and bad characters.
